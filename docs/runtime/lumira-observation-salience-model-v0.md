# Lumira Observation Salience Model v0

## Status

Draft

Experimental Runtime Concept

This document defines Observation Salience.

Observation Salience exists between:

* Observation Discovery
* Latent Formation

Its purpose is to identify prominence without introducing interpretation.

This document does not define:

* meaning
* importance
* reflection
* latent generation

---

# Core Principle

Observation answers:

> What can be observed?

Salience answers:

> What stands out?

These are different questions.

An observation may exist regardless of salience.

Salience affects prominence.

It does not determine existence.

---

# Purpose

Observation Discovery optimizes for preservation.

Observation Salience optimizes for orientation.

The goal of Salience is not to reduce the observation field.

The goal is to help later systems understand which observations are especially prominent within the dream.

---

# Observation Before Salience

The processing order is:

```text
Dream Material

↓

Observation Discovery

↓

Observation Set

↓

Observation Salience

↓

Latent Formation
```

Observations must exist before salience can be evaluated.

Salience is a property of an observation.

It is not part of observation discovery.

---

# Salience Is Not Importance

Salience should not be interpreted as:

* meaning
* significance
* truth
* relevance to life
* reflective value

Salience only describes how strongly an observation stands out within dream material.

An observation may have:

* low salience
* medium salience
* high salience

while remaining equally valid as an observation.

---

# Salience Is Not Ranking

The system should avoid treating salience as a single global ranking score.

Instead, salience should be understood as a profile.

Different observations may stand out for different reasons.

---

# Salience Dimensions

## Anomaly

Question:

> How unusual is this observation within ordinary reality?

Examples:

* impossible architecture
* missing reflection
* spatial distortion
* impossible transitions

High anomaly often creates high salience.

---

## Emotional Intensity

Question:

> How emotionally charged is the observation?

Examples:

* fear
* panic
* grief
* joy
* awe

Emotional intensity may increase salience.

---

## Agency Tension

Question:

> How strongly does the observation involve action, pursuit, resistance, searching, escape, control, or loss of control?

Examples:

* being chased
* searching for an exit
* attempting escape
* losing agency
* gaining agency

Agency tension may increase salience.

---

## Recurrence Potential

Question:

> Does the observation appear unusually central, repeated, or continuity-relevant within the dream?

Examples:

* repeated locations
* repeated figures
* repeated motifs

Recurrence potential may increase salience.

This is not a continuity claim.

It is only a prominence signal.

---

## Metacognitive Presence

Question:

> Does the observation involve awareness of dreaming or awareness of experience?

Examples:

* lucid realization
* reality testing
* dream awareness
* conscious dream control

Metacognitive observations are often highly salient.

---

# Example

Dream text:

> Egy végtelen folyosón futok, miközben a kijáratot keresem.

Observations:

1.

> Az álmodó fut.

2.

> Az álmodó kijáratot keres.

3.

> A tér végtelennek tűnik.

Possible salience profile:

```text
Az álmodó fut.

anomaly: low
emotional: low
agency: medium
recurrence: low
metacognitive: none


Az álmodó kijáratot keres.

anomaly: low
emotional: medium
agency: high
recurrence: medium
metacognitive: none


A tér végtelennek tűnik.

anomaly: high
emotional: low
agency: low
recurrence: low
metacognitive: none
```

All three observations remain preserved.

Salience does not remove observations.

---

# Relationship To Latent

Salience is not Latent.

Salience asks:

> Which observations stand out?

Latent asks:

> Which patterns connect observations?

Salience evaluates observations individually.

Latent evaluates relationships between observations.

---

# Relationship To Reflection

Salience is not Reflection.

High salience does not imply reflective importance.

Reflection may choose to engage with highly salient observations.

Reflection may also engage with low-salience observations.

Salience does not determine meaning.

---

# Preservation Principle

Observation should preserve.

Salience should weight.

Latent should organize.

Reflection should explore.

The stages should remain separate.

---

# Future Status

The Salience model should be considered experimental.

Its purpose is to support future Observation and Latent development.

The current runtime should prioritize:

1. Observation preservation
2. Observation quality
3. Observation multiplicity

before investing heavily in salience sophistication.

Salience exists to support preservation-based architecture.

It should never become a reason to discard observations.

---

# Final Principle

Observation asks:

> What is here?

Salience asks:

> What stands out?

Latent asks:

> What connects?

Reflection asks:

> What invites exploration?

These questions define four distinct cognitive layers within Lumira.
