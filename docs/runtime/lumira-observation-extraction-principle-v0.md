# Lumira Observation Extraction Principle v0

## Status

Draft — Observation Philosophy Layer

This document defines:

* the purpose of Observation extraction,
* the unit of Observation,
* extraction priorities,
* preservation principles,
* and the boundary between observation and downstream cognition.

This document is not:

* an ontology specification,
* a schema contract,
* a latent cognition design,
* a continuity model,
* or an interpretation framework.

Its purpose is to define how Observation should read dream material.

---

# 1. Core Principle

Observation is an act of noticing.

The goal of Observation is not to explain the dream.

The goal is not to determine meaning.

The goal is not to identify what matters most.

The goal is:

# to preserve what can be observed.

Observation exists to create a structured descriptive record of dream material before later systems perform continuity, salience, reflection, or orientation work.

---

# 2. Observation Is An Observer, Not A Classifier

Observation should not approach dream material as a classification task.

It should not ask:

* "What is this?"
* "Which category does this belong to?"
* "What is the best label?"

Instead it should ask:

* "What can be noticed here?"
* "What appears in this material?"
* "What is present, happening, or experienced?"

Classification exists only as a storage and organization step.

Observation precedes classification.

---

# 3. The Observation Unit

The canonical unit of Observation is:

# a descriptive observation.

Not:

* a dream fragment,
* a scene,
* a category,
* or a schema entry.

An observation is a single bounded noticing that can be supported by dream evidence.

Examples:

* a person appears
* a location is present
* movement occurs
* fear is experienced
* a transition happens
* agency becomes blocked
* space behaves impossibly

Observations are evidence-linked.

---

# 4. One Dream Element May Produce Multiple Observations

A single dream fragment may support multiple observations.

Example:

> "I walk through an endless hallway while searching for an exit."

Possible observations:

* location present
* movement occurs
* searching behavior occurs
* agency constraint appears
* spatial instability appears

Observation extraction should not assume a one-to-one relationship between dream material and categories.

Multiple valid observations may emerge from the same evidence.

---

# 5. Preservation Before Reduction

Observation should optimize for:

# preservation.

Not:

# compression.

The extractor should prefer preserving relevant descriptive material rather than prematurely reducing it into a smaller summary.

Later systems may:

* prioritize,
* connect,
* suppress,
* weight,
* or surface.

Observation should primarily preserve.

---

# 6. Observation Layers

Observation may preserve material across multiple descriptive layers.

These include:

## Structure

What is present?

Examples:

* scenes
* actors
* objects
* locations

## Relations

What happens?

Examples:

* interactions
* transitions
* affective changes

## Phenomenology

How is the dream experienced?

Examples:

* emotions
* body states
* agency states
* metacognitive moments
* dream-state qualities
* atmospheres

## Continuity Seams

What may matter later?

Examples:

* recurrence candidates
* continuity fragments

These layers coexist because Observation is a descriptive substrate rather than a single-level ontology.

---

# 7. Observation Does Not Decide Importance

Observation should not determine:

* reflective importance
* continuity importance
* thread identity
* user relevance
* opening eligibility
* reflective priority

Those responsibilities belong to downstream systems.

Observation preserves candidate material.

It does not decide what should become foreground.

---

# 8. Observation Does Not Interpret

Observation may describe:

* what appears,
* what happens,
* what changes,
* how experience unfolds.

Observation may not claim:

* meaning,
* symbolism,
* diagnosis,
* psychological truth,
* hidden motivation,
* reflective conclusions.

Observation answers:

> What appears here?

Not:

> What does this mean?

---

# 9. Extraction Heuristic

When reading a dream, Observation should proceed in the following order:

1. Notice what appears.
2. Notice what happens.
3. Notice how it is experienced.
4. Notice what changes.
5. Notice what may be worth preserving for continuity.
6. Only then organize observations into schema categories.

The schema should organize observations.

The schema should not determine what is observed.

---

# 10. Final Principle

A successful Observation extraction leaves the dream richer than a summary and more structured than raw text.

Its purpose is to preserve descriptive possibility.

Not to reduce ambiguity.

Not to determine meaning.

Not to decide relevance.

Observation exists so that later reflective systems have something trustworthy to work with.
