# Lumira Summary/Re-entry Owner Approval Criteria v0

## 1. Why Owner Approval Matters

Summary/re-entry surfaces set the emotional entry posture of Lumira.  
Technical correctness (valid payloads, deterministic ordering, parity pass) is necessary but insufficient.

Owner approval is required because these surfaces directly shape:
- perceived continuity authority
- pressure vs spaciousness
- interpretive boundary safety
- trust in Lumira's companionship posture

Non-negotiable principle:
- correct runtime behavior alone is not enough for rollout readiness.

## 2. Lumira Feel Criteria (Critical Section)

A candidate summary/re-entry behavior is approvable only if it still feels like Lumira:
- calm: no urgency signaling, no escalation framing
- spacious: bounded foreground and visible breathing room
- companionship: observational and invitational, not directive
- non-authoritative: no meaning ownership by system
- optional: user can ignore, defer, or leave unresolved safely
- orientation-first: helps user locate context before deepening
- emotionally non-coercive: no guilt/obligation dynamics
- silence-legitimate: no forced resurfacing
- non-workflow: no "resume unfinished tasks" posture

Owner review question:
- "Would a low-energy returning user feel gently oriented, not pushed?"

## 3. Orientational Layer Acceptance Criteria

Orientational layer covers:
- overview/synthesis surfaces
- associative continuity maps
- exploratory adjacency browsing

Acceptable:
- somewhat higher informational density than deep reflection
- broader continuity visibility when clearly tiered
- associative adjacency if bounded and non-insistent
- exploratory pattern visibility with explicit uncertainty posture

Still prohibited:
- interpretive certainty
- emotional coercion
- pseudo-therapy framing
- synthetic significance inflation
- forced coherence claims

Density rule for orientation:
- rich is acceptable; pressuring is not.

## 4. Deep Reflection Layer Acceptance Criteria

Deep reflection layer covers:
- focused reflection surfaces
- processing-oriented continuity
- single-thread engagement posture

Required:
- low foreground density
- bounded invitations (target 0-1, hard cap 2)
- omission-first when evidence is weak or conflicting
- explicit silence legitimacy
- emotional spaciousness over completeness

Forbidden:
- stacked unresolved cues
- continuity chaining that feels compulsory
- multi-thread pressure in foreground

## 5. Density Approval Criteria

Owner evaluates density by felt pressure, not raw count alone.

Approval heuristics:
- acceptable informational richness:
  - layered context exists but user attention is not pulled in multiple directions at once
- unacceptable emotional pressure density:
  - concurrent cues create urgency, obligation, or "too much to hold"

Layer distinction:
- orientational layer may show broader context if visibly demoted and bounded
- deep reflection layer must remain narrow, center-led, and low-pressure

Automatic no-go density signals:
- repeated simultaneous invitations
- unresolved-pressure stacking
- continuity flooding beyond contract caps

## 6. Interpretive Boundary Criteria (Critical Section)

Acceptable reflective orientation:
- evidence-linked continuity hints
- uncertainty-aware pattern visibility
- provisional framing that preserves ambiguity

Unacceptable interpretive drift:
- "your dream means..."
- psychological certainty claims
- emotional diagnosis
- hidden therapeutic authority posture
- certainty framing from latent/recurrence alone
- forced narrative coherence

Owner approval test:
- "Does this orient the user, or interpret the user?"

## 7. Emotional Pressure Criteria

No-go experiential outcomes:
- unfinished-task feeling
- emotional obligation to continue
- resurfacing pressure loops
- continuity guilt
- "the system wants me to continue"
- pressure to resolve motifs/themes now

Approval requires evidence that:
- invitations remain optional
- deferral is respected without penalty tone
- return posture stays gentle even with unresolved continuity present

## 8. Silence / Omission Approval Criteria

Silence and omission are first-class healthy outcomes.

Approve only when:
- non-surfacing is possible and exercised under ambiguity/saturation
- dormant continuity can remain dormant
- suppressed/deferred lines stay out of active foreground
- "nothing surfaced today" is treated as valid Lumira behavior

Disapprove if:
- system repeatedly fills space with weak continuity
- omission is effectively unavailable in high-risk moments

## 9. Rollout Approval Requirements

Before any guarded summary/re-entry route switch planning, require:
- parity evidence pack (summary + re-entry dry-run results)
- rollback proof (immediate disablement, legacy path intact)
- latest drift review outcomes
- density/calmness review with explicit pass rationale
- suppression/defer/silence review
- owner walkthrough on representative payload examples

Required pre-rollout checklist:
- [ ] parity evidence reviewed
- [ ] rollback rehearsal accepted
- [ ] suppression/defer parity accepted
- [ ] density/pacing accepted per layer
- [ ] interpretive boundary accepted
- [ ] no-go examples not observed
- [ ] owner sign-off recorded

## 10. No-go Examples (Critical Section)

Block rollout if any of these are observed:
- summary feels diagnostic or interpretively certain
- continuity feels compulsive rather than optional
- too many simultaneous invitations in entry view
- emotionally loaded resurfacing dominates orientation
- latent continuity over-foregrounded without user-grounded evidence
- synthetic coherence ("too neat" story compression)
- "AI knows your unconscious better than you do" feeling
- deferred/suppressed material reappears as pressure cue

## 11. Expansion Philosophy

Summary/re-entry expansion must be slower than B1/B2 because emotional impact is higher.

Governance posture:
- gradual, bounded experiments
- one high-risk surface step at a time
- explicit owner checkpoint before each escalation
- reversibility prioritized over coverage

Rule:
- no automatic expansion from successful lower-risk switches.
