# Lumira Unified Reflective Space Rollout Plan v0

## 1. Why Reflective Space Must Be Unified

Summary, re-entry, and session entry cannot mature as separate reflective products without fragmenting Lumira's core experience.

Fragmentation risk:
- inconsistent emotional posture across routes
- contradictory pacing and density behavior
- route-local optimization that erodes reflective coherence
- continuity feeling "owned by surfaces" instead of held by one reflective space

Unified direction:
- Reflective Space is the canonical reflective entry environment.
- Context changes emphasis, not product identity.

## 2. Canonical Reflective Space Definition

Reflective Space is Lumira's single continuity-oriented environment where users:
- orient to current continuity state
- notice bounded adjacent context
- optionally deepen into one active reflective center
- return without obligation

Core role:
- companionship over authority
- orientation over interpretation
- calm return posture over completion pressure

## 3. Entry vs Re-entry vs Summary Posture

Same space, different postures:

- First entry:
  - emphasis: orientation scaffold + minimal pressure
  - behavior: establish center softly, low invitation density

- Returning after absence:
  - emphasis: gentle continuity re-anchor
  - behavior: bounded center + ambient hints, suppression-aware surfacing

- Active continuity revisit:
  - emphasis: center continuity and selective deepening
  - behavior: preserve momentum without workflow tone

- Sparse session:
  - emphasis: minimal mode validity
  - behavior: silence/omission allowed; "nothing surfaced" acceptable

- High-density symbolic session:
  - emphasis: layered orientation with strict caps
  - behavior: demotion before expansion, no flood surfacing

## 4. Orientational Layer Within Reflective Space

Orientational layer role:
- provide map-like continuity orientation
- support bounded associative exploration
- surface rich but non-pressuring context

Behavior rules:
- layered continuity hierarchy must stay visible (foreground/midground/background)
- broader visibility is acceptable only with bounded density and explicit demotion
- movement to deep reflection is optional and user-led

## 5. Deep Reflection Layer Within Reflective Space

Deep reflection role:
- hold single-center reflective attention
- support emotionally spacious engagement
- keep invitation pacing low and optional

Behavior rules:
- one gravitational center
- low foreground density
- bounded invitations (target 0-1, cap 2)
- omission-first under ambiguity
- silence legitimacy preserved

## 6. Transition Grammar Between Layers (Critical Section)

Orientational -> deep reflection:
- transition only when a center is clear or explicitly chosen
- demote peripheral continuity before deepening
- preserve user agency ("may continue", not "should continue")

Deep reflection -> orientational:
- allow widening context without pressure
- return to layered map view, not progression state

Transition safeguards:
- no abrupt escalation
- no workflow-stage feeling
- no pressure-based promotions
- no continuity flooding during transitions

Pacing rule:
- orient, invite, deepen only on user pull.

## 7. Route / API Convergence Strategy

Current surfaces:
- `/session/[id]` (session overview/re-entry-like)
- `/session/[id]/summary` (high-coupling summary surface)
- `/api/session-summary` (aggregate DTO source)

Convergence strategy:
- treat current routes as temporary delivery shells for one Reflective Space model
- align all route behavior to unified layer grammar rather than route-specific semantics
- use one canonical reflective payload shape as convergence target

Transitional coexistence allowances:
- temporary route duplication is allowed while parity and rollback gates remain active
- route-local guards remain valid during convergence

Eventual canonical direction:
- one canonical Reflective Space read model
- route distinctions become presentation/context entrypoints, not separate reflective systems

Rollback posture:
- maintain legacy path reversibility until unified reflective behavior is proven stable

## 8. Legacy Assumption Reconciliation (Critical Section)

Outdated assumptions to revise:
- "summary rollout" and "re-entry rollout" as independent long-term product tracks
- route-first ownership of reflective behavior

Updated assumption:
- summary/re-entry/entry are contextual expressions of one Reflective Space.

What remains authoritative:
- interaction grammar constraints
- owner approval criteria
- suppression/calmness invariants
- rollback-first governance

Controlled supersession rule:
- where older route-separated plans conflict with unified-space coherence, unified direction takes precedence.
- supersession must be explicit and documented, not implicit drift.

## 9. Rollout Sequencing

Recommended sequence:
1. presentation-layer validation
2. wording/tone validation
3. continuity hierarchy validation
4. guarded Reflective Space slice rollout
5. owner walkthrough/review
6. broader reflective convergence
7. legacy route reduction

Sequencing philosophy:
- bounded experiments
- owner-gated high-risk surfaces
- no automatic expansion from local success

## 10. Reflective Space Failure Modes

No-go patterns:
- reflective feed behavior (infinite continuity stream feel)
- continuity flooding
- route fragmentation (different reflective personalities by route)
- emotional overload
- pseudo-therapy drift
- over-centralized continuity authority
- compulsive re-entry loops

Early warnings:
- frequent dense payloads with low engagement
- repeated defer/suppress violations
- route-specific tone divergence

## 11. Governance Implications

Future tickets must treat:
- summary
- re-entry
- session entry

as one coherence domain with contextual emphasis variants.

Governance update:
- reflective experience coherence takes priority over route-local optimization.
- route/API work must prove grammar alignment, not only technical parity.
- owner review remains required before broadening high-influence surfaces.
