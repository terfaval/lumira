# VALIDATION - Summary/Re-entry Owner Walkthrough Packet

Date: 2026-05-17  
Scope: owner-facing experiential review packet for reflective summary/re-entry behavior  
Mode: validation/audit preparation only (no route switch, no ownership transfer)

## 1. Walkthrough Purpose

This packet prepares explicit owner review before any guarded summary/re-entry rollout planning.

Why this walkthrough is required:
- technical parity and deterministic payload behavior are necessary, but not sufficient
- summary/re-entry shapes emotional entry posture and perceived system authority
- rollout readiness depends on felt experience, not only structural correctness

Primary walkthrough question:
- "Does this still feel like Lumira?"

## 2. How To Review The Packet

Review each scenario twice:
1. first pass for "what is shown"
2. second pass for "how it feels"

For each pass, evaluate:
- calmness: does the payload reduce urgency?
- pacing: does it invite rather than push?
- density: is this rich or overloaded?
- invitation pressure: do openings feel optional?
- interpretive tone: does it orient or interpret?
- emotional spaciousness: is there room to do nothing?
- orientational usefulness: does it help user locate themselves?
- deep reflection restraint: does focus stay bounded?

Scoring suggestion:
- `PASS`
- `PASS WITH CONTAINMENT NOTE`
- `NO-GO`

## 4. Orientational Layer Walkthrough

Owner should validate orientational richness on:
- continuity visibility breadth
- adjacency clarity
- discovery support
- cognitive comprehensibility

Expected acceptable posture:
- broader than deep reflection
- bounded and tiered
- non-compulsive

Review prompt:
- "Does this feel rich but non-pressuring?"

## 5. Deep Reflection Layer Walkthrough

Owner should validate deep reflection restraint on:
- single-center focus
- low invitation count
- omission under ambiguity
- silence legitimacy
- emotional spaciousness

Expected acceptable posture:
- calm deepening
- optional engagement
- no compulsive continuation cue

Review prompt:
- "Could the user safely pause here without feeling pushed?"

## 6. Density Evaluation Packet

Acceptable density examples:
- orientational surface shows multiple context layers, but only one clear center
- additional continuity cues are peripheral and dismissible

Excessive density examples:
- multiple simultaneous invitations competing in foreground
- unresolved lines stacked without demotion
- continuity cues that collectively imply urgency

Heuristic distinction:
- informational richness:
  - "I can see context."
- emotional pressure:
  - "I feel I must act now."

Owner no-go threshold:
- if richness repeatedly converts into obligation feeling, reject rollout readiness.

## 7. Interpretive Boundary Packet (Critical Section)

Acceptable reflective orientation examples:
- "Here are nearby continuity anchors."
- "This may connect to prior motifs."
- uncertainty preserved, no certainty tone

Unacceptable drift examples:
- pseudo-therapy framing
- "AI knows you" affect
- synthetic significance inflation from recurrence alone
- hidden authority tone from latent-heavy composition
- forced coherence narrative

Owner boundary test:
- "Does this support meaning-making, or take it over?"

## 8. Silence / Omission Packet

Positive restraint examples:
- zero active opening when confidence is weak
- deferred/suppressed lines remain unsurfaced
- dormant continuity stays ambient/dormant
- fewer surfaced elements improve calmness and orientation quality

Required owner acceptance:
- "nothing surfaced today" remains a valid Lumira outcome.

## 9. No-go Experience Packet

Blockers for rollout planning:
- compulsive continuation feeling
- continuity flooding
- unresolved-pressure escalation
- interpretive overreach
- synthetic coherence that feels overconfident
- emotional coercion or guilt dynamics

Immediate no-go trigger:
- user would likely feel "the system wants me to continue" instead of "I can return gently."
