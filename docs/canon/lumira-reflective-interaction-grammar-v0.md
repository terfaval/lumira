# Lumira Reflective Interaction Grammar v0

## 1. Why Lumira Needs An Interaction Grammar

Runtime architecture defines what can be surfaced.  
Interaction grammar defines how it is experienced.

Without explicit grammar, reflective runtime can drift into:
- workflow behavior
- productivity pressure
- interpretive authority
- pseudo-therapy tone

Lumira interaction stance is non-negotiable:
- not a workflow system
- not a productivity engine
- not a therapeutic authority
- not an interpretation machine

The grammar exists to keep reflective continuity expressive, bounded, and emotionally safe.

## 2. Core Reflective Interaction Principles (Critical Section)

Core grammar principles:
- orientation over interpretation
- projection as possibility surface, not reflective truth
- continuity without possession
- return without obligation
- rich but non-pressuring
- silence is a valid outcome
- invitation over insistence
- emotional spaciousness over completion
- omission under ambiguity
- companionship over authority
- mystery preservation over elegant coherence
- attentional pacing over insight delivery

Operational reading:
- when uncertain, surface less
- when dense, demote rather than stack
- when continuity competes, keep one gravitational center
- adjacency is suggestive, never proof
- recurrence is a continuity hint, never psychological verdict

## 3. Orientational Layer Grammar

Orientational layer covers:
- overview/synthesis surfaces
- continuity maps
- associative adjacency
- discovery support

Permitted behavior:
- broader continuity visibility
- layered context (foreground/midground/background)
- bounded associative exploration
- informational richness with explicit tiering

Required behavior:
- map-like orientation first
- optional movement into deeper focus
- uncertainty-preserving framing

Prohibited behavior:
- urgency framing
- compulsive continuation cues
- interpretive certainty
- emotional coercion

Orientational grammar rule:
- rich is acceptable; pressuring is not.
- adjacent is acceptable; interpretively collapsed is not.

## 4. Deep Reflection Layer Grammar

Deep reflection layer covers:
- focused reflection
- single-thread engagement
- emotionally attentive deepening

Required behavior:
- one gravitational center
- low foreground density
- bounded invitations (target 0-1, hard cap 2)
- silence legitimacy
- optional engagement at all times

Required transitions:
- broader context must be demoted before deepening
- unresolved continuity must not auto-escalate into pressure

Prohibited behavior:
- multi-thread foreground competition
- stacked unresolved prompts
- "continue now" pressure loops

## 5. Continuity Presentation Hierarchy (Critical Section)

Continuity hierarchy is mandatory:

- Foreground:
  - active now, center-linked, high relevance
  - interaction implication: can be engaged now

- Midground:
  - near-center, revisitable, adjacent continuity
  - interaction implication: available, not demanding

- Background:
  - dormant, historical, low-confidence, suppressed/deferred context
  - interaction implication: remains quiet unless explicitly reactivated

Hierarchy rules:
- continuity must be layered, not flattened
- background cannot silently promote itself
- midground supports orientation, not pressure

## 6. Density Grammar

Density is evaluated by felt pressure, not count alone.

Acceptable orientational density:
- broader context visible
- one clear center remains legible
- adjacency is bounded and demoted

Acceptable deep reflection density:
- narrow focus
- minimal concurrent invitations
- explicit breathing room

Escalation limits:
- no continuity flooding
- no linear surfacing with signal count
- no unresolved-pressure stacking

Hard distinction:
- cognitive richness: "I can see context."
- emotional obligation: "I must act now."

Interpretive distinction:
- adjacency does not imply meaning
- recurrence does not imply hidden truth
- coherence should remain provisional, not conclusive

## 7. Wording / Tone Grammar

Tone requirements:
- observational
- invitational
- uncertainty-aware
- companion-like, never directive

Allowed wording posture examples:
- "You may return to this thread."
- "This might connect to earlier motifs."
- "If useful, we can stay with this."

Prohibited wording posture:
- interpretive certainty ("this means...")
- therapeutic certainty
- hidden authority tone
- "AI knows you" language
- compulsive continuation language

Uncertainty grammar:
- use provisional framing (`may`, `might`, `possible`)
- avoid verdict framing (`is`, `proves`, `definitely`)
- avoid coherence framing that implies closure ("this finally explains everything")

## 8. CTA / Invitation Grammar

CTA behavior must preserve user agency.

Required CTA posture:
- optional and reversible
- low-pressure
- context-linked

Preferred grammar:
- "you may continue"
- "if now feels right"
- "we can leave this here"

Prohibited grammar:
- "you should continue"
- "finish this"
- "resolve this now"

Interaction rule:
- invitations are pathways, not obligations.

## 9. Silence / Omission Grammar

Silence is a positive reflective behavior, not a failure state.

Use omission when:
- confidence is weak
- signals conflict
- suppression/defer is active
- density is near cap
- user bandwidth appears low

Required preservation:
- dormant continuity may remain dormant
- "nothing surfaced today" is valid Lumira behavior

Silence grammar:
- less can be more reflective.

## 10. Emotional Pacing Grammar (Critical Section)

Pacing rhythm:
1. orient
2. invite
3. deepen only on user pull

Pacing requirements:
- interaction breathing room between cues
- bounded resurfacing cadence
- stable center before introducing adjacency
- demotion before expansion

Must avoid:
- emotional stacking
- continuity flooding
- unresolved-pressure escalation
- optimizing for "insight delivery velocity" over reflective attention quality

Cadence rule:
- return should feel like re-entry, not reactivation pressure.

## 11. Anti-pattern Grammar

Explicit anti-patterns:
- workflowification
- compulsive continuation
- pseudo-therapy framing
- interpretive authority drift
- synthetic significance inflation
- continuity guilt
- emotional coercion
- flattening all continuity into foreground

Danger signals:
- repeated invitations despite defer/silence
- dense multi-source continuity with no demotion
- language implying privileged psychological knowledge
- overly elegant coherence that erases dream ambiguity/fragmentation

## 12. Rollout Implications

This grammar constrains rollout planning:
- guarded switches must validate grammar conformance, not only payload parity
- owner review must include experiential checks against this grammar
- presentation-layer validation must include density, tone, and pressure checks
- rollback-first posture remains mandatory for summary/re-entry surfaces

Governance implication:
- rollout is blocked if grammar no-go patterns appear, even if technical checks pass.
