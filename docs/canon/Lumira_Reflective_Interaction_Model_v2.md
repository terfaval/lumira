# Lumira Reflective Interaction Model v2

## Purpose

This document defines the evolving interaction model of Lumira’s Reflective Space.

It consolidates prior UX, IA, and reflective-flow discussions into a single conceptual model describing:

* how reflective dream work unfolds,
* how users interact with dream material,
* how reflective depth emerges,
* how threads, highlights, and glossary continuity function,
* and how Lumira behaves as a contemplative companion rather than a workflow engine.

This document is:

* conceptual,
* interaction-focused,
* implementation-agnostic.

It is NOT:

* a component specification,
* a visual design system,
* or a finalized technical architecture.

---

# 1. Core experiential model

Lumira consists of two fundamentally different experiential spaces:

## 1. Capture Space

Purpose:

* rapid dream capture before memory fades.

Characteristics:

* minimal
* immediate
* low-friction
* text-first
* almost tool-less
* no analysis pressure
* no reflective overload

Dream capture alone is a successful session.

---

## 2. Reflective Space

Purpose:

* sustained engagement with the dream after capture.

Reflective Space is:

* exploratory,
* reflective,
* memory-oriented,
* continuity-aware,
* and gradually deepening.

The user should feel:

> “I am staying with this dream.”

not:

> “I am progressing through a workflow.”

---

# 2. Reflective Space philosophy

Reflective Space is not:

* a dashboard,
* a wizard flow,
* a coaching funnel,
* a task system,
* or a chatbot interface.

It is:

# a reflective dream workspace.

The system:

* holds attention,
* preserves continuity,
* surfaces openings,
* and supports reflective responding.

The system should never:

* force interpretation,
* force progression,
* or visually dominate the dream itself.

---

# 3. Dream Surface model

The Dream Surface is the central substrate of Reflective Space.

It contains:

* the canonical raw dream,
* highlights,
* notes,
* reflective anchors,
* continuity markers,
* and attached reflective threads.

The raw dream remains:

# the source material.

AI-generated content:

* cannot replace it,
* cannot override it,
* and cannot become the primary narrative truth.

---

## AI orientation layer

Lumira may generate:

* summaries,
* frame/context,
* orientation text,
* latent cues,
* or reflective openings.

These belong to:

# the Orientation Layer.

Their purpose is:

* cognitive orientation,
* not authoritative interpretation.

Orientation content should:

* help re-enter the dream,
* reduce cognitive load,
* and surface possible areas of engagement.

It must remain:

* collapsible,
* secondary,
* and visibly distinct from the canonical dream material.

---

# 4. Reflective modes

Reflective Space contains two major reflective modes.

These are:

* interaction states,
* not necessarily separate routes.

---

## A. Orientation Layer

Purpose:

# “Where does this dream currently lead?”

This mode:

* summarizes the reflective landscape,
* surfaces active areas,
* and reveals continuity.

The Orientation Layer acts as:

# a reflective map.

It may contain:

* highlights,
* unresolved motifs,
* recurring symbols,
* glossary continuity,
* open reflective threads,
* revisited areas,
* reflective invitations,
* dormant lines of thought,
* continuity signals,
* and thread activity.

The user should feel:

* oriented,
* invited,
* and aware of reflective possibilities.

But not:

* pressured,
* overloaded,
* or workflow-constrained.

---

## B. Deep Reflection Layer

Purpose:

# “Stay with this.”

This mode narrows attention around:

* one thread,
* one motif,
* one reflective opening,
* one unresolved area,
* or one active response process.

The Deep Reflection Layer should:

* reduce peripheral complexity,
* maintain context,
* and prioritize reflective writing.

It should still preserve:

* local continuity,
* relevant excerpts,
* related responses,
* and nearby reflective context.

But:

# focus becomes dominant.

---

# 5. Reflective responding

The primary interaction primitive of Lumira is:

# reflective responding.

Not:

* navigation,
* highlighting,
* clicking,
* or prompt progression.

Reflective responding includes:

* answering a reflective opening,
* writing a note,
* attaching meaning,
* revisiting a motif,
* continuing an unresolved thought,
* or expanding a prior response.

The system supports:

* sustained reflective engagement,
* not rapid task completion.

---

# 6. Highlights as reflective anchors

Highlights are:

# user-owned salience markers.

They are not:

* decorations,
* passive annotations,
* or automatic semantic labels.

Highlights represent:

* attention,
* emotional density,
* uncertainty,
* recurrence,
* importance,
* or reflective interest.

Each highlight may connect to:

* notes,
* glossary motifs,
* reflective threads,
* continuity signals,
* related dreams,
* or revisitation states.

---

## AI-generated highlights

AI may suggest highlights.

However:

* user confirmation is required before persistence,
* and the system must never silently elevate semantic importance.

---

# 7. Glossary philosophy

Glossary is:

# personal motif memory.

It is NOT:

* a universal dream dictionary,
* an interpretation engine,
* or symbolic truth authority.

Glossary exists to support:

* continuity,
* recurrence,
* personal association,
* and cross-session memory.

Glossary entries may contain:

* user notes,
* recurrence history,
* associated dreams,
* reflective connections,
* and linked highlights.

Glossary signals should:

* gently surface continuity,
* not impose meaning.

---

# 8. Reflective threads

Reflective threads are:

# the primary continuity structure of reflective work.

A thread may begin from:

* a highlight,
* a motif,
* a reflective opening,
* an unresolved tension,
* a prior response,
* or manual user initiation.

Threads are:

* revisitable,
* non-linear,
* and continuity-aware.

---

## Thread lifecycle

Threads may exist in states such as:

* open
* deferred
* answered
* revisited
* dormant

Threads are not tasks.

They are:

# reflective trajectories.

---

## Thread navigation

Users should increasingly navigate:

# through reflective threads,

not:

# through rigid route progression.

A thread acts as:

* memory,
* continuity,
* and reflective orientation.

The user should feel:

* “I can return to this line of thought”
  rather than:
* “I completed this step.”

---

# 9. Reflective topology

Reflective Space should gradually form:

# a topology of thought and memory.

This may include:

* connected motifs,
* recurring highlights,
* unresolved clusters,
* revisited themes,
* continuity patterns,
* reflective density,
* and dormant areas.

These structures should emerge:

* gradually,
* quietly,
* and peripherally.

Never:

* as analytics dashboards,
* productivity metrics,
* or gamified systems.

---

# 10. Instrument philosophy

Lumira may feel like:

# a contemplative dream instrument.

But:
this metaphor must emerge through:

* behavior,
* continuity,
* pacing,
* and subtle signals.

NOT through:

* fake machinery,
* retro skeuomorphism,
* sci-fi HUDs,
* or decorative complexity.

---

## Instrument signals

Instrument-like signals may indicate:

* recurrence,
* unresolved states,
* continuity activity,
* revisitation,
* emotional density,
* or reflective activation.

Signals should behave like:

* quiet laboratory indicators,
* subtle resonance cues,
* or peripheral status lights.

Never:

* dominant visual spectacle.

---

# 11. Density philosophy

Reflective Space should preserve:

# calm density.

By default:

* only the most relevant reflective structures remain visible.

The system should:

* progressively reveal depth,
* collapse inactive areas,
* and reduce simultaneous visual competition.

---

## Visible by default

Typically:

* dream surface,
* active highlights,
* active/open threads,
* one reflective invitation maximum,
* lightweight continuity cues.

---

## Expandable on intent

Examples:

* full glossary continuity,
* archived responses,
* thread history,
* recurrence maps,
* deferred reflections,
* related dreams,
* deeper reflective context.

---

# 12. Mobile philosophy

Mobile use is central to Lumira.

Reflective Space must support:

* low-energy cognition,
* half-awake states,
* fragmented sessions,
* and soft re-entry.

Mobile UX should:

* minimize persistent chrome,
* prioritize thumb ergonomics,
* avoid dense simultaneous panels,
* and prefer layered focus states.

Bottom sheets, focus modes, and contextual expansion are preferred over:

* dashboard compression,
* persistent sidebars,
* or desktop-style panel duplication.

---

# 13. Non-interpretive contract

Lumira remains:

# non-interpretive by design.

The system may:

* surface patterns,
* preserve continuity,
* generate reflective openings,
* or highlight potential areas of attention.

But it must never:

* claim symbolic truth,
* impose psychological interpretation,
* or overwrite user meaning.

The user remains:

# the final authority of meaning.

---

# 14. Route vs state philosophy

Technical routes may remain for implementation reasons.

However:
the experiential model increasingly treats:

* Frame
* Direction
* Work
* Summary
* Highlights

as:

# states/layers of the same Reflective Space.

Long-term mappings:

| Legacy concept | Reflective Space interpretation |
| -------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Frame          | orientation layer               |
| Direction      | attention lens                  |
| Work           | reflective thread engagement    |
| Summary        | reflective workspace default    |
| Highlights     | reflective anchors              |
| Glossary       | continuity/memory layer         |

---

# remaining in relationship with a dream.
