# Purpose

This document defines:

* the major categories of Reflective Opportunities
* why Opportunities attract reflective attention
* how reflective salience should be understood
* how credibility and reflective potential interact

This document does not define:

* Opening generation
* Thread implementation
* Reflection implementation
* scoring systems
* implementation details

---

# Core Principle

Opportunity Types are not ontological categories.

Opportunity Types are Reflective Attention Categories.

They do not answer:

> What is this?

They answer:

> Why did the Latent layer notice this?

The purpose of the taxonomy is not classification for its own sake.

The purpose is to explain why a reflective structure may deserve attention.

---

# Opportunity Salience

Not all Opportunities deserve equal attention.

Reflective salience describes how strongly an Opportunity stands out within the reflective landscape.

Salience does not represent truth.

Salience does not represent certainty.

Salience represents reflective significance.

---

# Dual Salience Principle

Reflective salience emerges from two independent dimensions:

```text
Credibility
+
Reflective Potential
```

---

## Credibility

Credibility answers:

> How well supported is this Opportunity?

Credibility emerges from evidence.

Examples include:

* dream evidence
* observation evidence
* continuity evidence
* thread evidence
* reflection evidence

Higher credibility means stronger grounding.

Higher credibility does not imply correctness.

---

## Reflective Potential

Reflective Potential answers:

> How promising is this Opportunity for reflective exploration?

Reflective Potential emerges from qualities such as:

* tension
* contradiction
* uncertainty
* transformation
* recurrence
* novelty
* incompleteness
* ambiguity

Higher Reflective Potential means greater possibility for exploration.

Higher Reflective Potential does not imply correctness.

---

# Salience Balance Principle

Neither dimension is sufficient alone.

High Credibility with low Reflective Potential may produce flat Opportunities.

High Reflective Potential with low Credibility may produce speculative Opportunities.

The Latent layer should prefer Opportunities that demonstrate both grounding and openness.

---

# Silence Principle

The Latent layer is not obligated to generate Opportunities.

Reflective silence is a legitimate outcome.

The system should not manufacture Opportunities simply to satisfy a target quantity.

When evidence and reflective potential are weak, silence may be preferable.

---

# Opportunity Categories

The following categories represent major forms of reflective attention.

They are not mutually exclusive.

An Opportunity may belong to multiple categories simultaneously.

---

# Structural Categories

Structural categories emerge from observed relationships and patterns.

---

## Pattern

Something appears repeatedly.

Examples:

* recurring structures
* recurring situations
* recurring configurations

Reflective question:

> What continues to return?

---

## Continuity

Something connects across dreams, reflections, or contexts.

Reflective question:

> What remains connected?

---

## Relationship

Meaningful relationships appear between elements.

Reflective question:

> What appears connected?

---

# Dynamic Categories

Dynamic categories emerge from movement and change.

---

## Transition

Something changes state.

Examples:

```text
Exploration
↓
Danger
```

```text
Trust
↓
Threat
```

Reflective question:

> What is changing?

---

## Transformation

A structure changes form over time.

Reflective question:

> What is becoming something else?

---

## Reversal

An expected direction is inverted.

Examples:

```text
Protection
↓
Danger
```

```text
Threat
↓
Protection
```

Reflective question:

> What has changed direction?

---

# Tension Categories

Tension categories emerge from instability.

---

## Tension

Two forces, states, or possibilities coexist.

Reflective question:

> What appears unresolved?

---

## Contradiction

Elements appear incompatible.

Reflective question:

> What does not fit together?

---

## Ambiguity

Multiple plausible readings coexist.

Reflective question:

> What remains uncertain?

---

# Open Categories

Open categories emerge from incompleteness.

---

## Gap

Something appears missing.

Reflective question:

> What is absent?

---

## Unresolved Pattern

A structure appears incomplete.

Reflective question:

> What remains unfinished?

---

## Unknown

The Latent layer recognizes significance but cannot confidently categorize it.

Reflective question:

> What is difficult to describe?

---

# Attentional Categories

Attentional categories emerge from salience itself.

---

## Curiosity

Something naturally attracts reflective attention.

Reflective question:

> Why does this stand out?

---

## Novelty

Something differs from established patterns.

Reflective question:

> What is different here?

---

## Salience Signal

An element appears unusually prominent without a clear explanation.

Reflective question:

> Why is this so noticeable?

---

# Primary and Secondary Categories

An Opportunity may have multiple categories.

The Latent layer should identify:

* Primary Category
* Secondary Categories

Example:

```text
Primary:
Transition

Secondary:
Tension
Curiosity
```

Another example:

```text
Primary:
Continuity

Secondary:
Contradiction
```

The purpose is to preserve nuance without forcing a single interpretation.

---

# Category Independence Principle

Categories do not determine meaning.

Two Opportunities may share the same category while leading to completely different reflective exploration.

Categories describe attention.

They do not describe truth.

---

# Reflection Influence

Reflection material may increase salience.

Not because it proves an Opportunity.

But because it represents user-generated reflective evidence.

Reflection-derived material should be treated as highly significant within the credibility dimension.

---

# Continuity Influence

Continuity information may increase salience.

Examples include:

* recurring glossary entities
* recurring dream structures
* recurring relationships
* recurring Opportunity manifestations

Continuity may strengthen credibility.

Continuity alone does not guarantee reflective importance.

---

# Final Principle

Reflective Opportunities are not important because they are true.

Reflective Opportunities are important because they may help organize meaningful exploration.

A healthy Opportunity Taxonomy should help the Latent layer notice what deserves attention without deciding what it means.
